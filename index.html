§03 — Oregon Market Reality

Who's actually paying, and for what.

This isn't theory. These are crops and acres being sprayed by drone in Oregon right now, with active demand exceeding supply in two specific windows.

The Willamette Valley is the obvious entry point. Hazelnuts are the killer crop — preventive Eastern Filbert Blight sprays in March–April when ground rigs literally can't get into the orchard because of mud. Several thousand acres of demand, fast-growing because of the new EFB-resistant varieties going in.

Vineyards are real but tricky — Willamette Pinot blocks are small (20–100ac), trellised tight, and want premium drift control. Premium pricing ($22–35/ac) but lower throughput. Adelsheim is already piloting drones.

The under-the-radar play is grass seed and clover in the South Valley — winter herbicide passes when fields are too wet to mud through with a ground rig. That's where Pratum is doing most of their volume.

Eastern Oregon wheat is the long play if you want broad-acre throughput. Less competition, bigger fields, lower per-acre but way better daily revenue.

§04 — Oregon's Catch-22

The real bottleneck, and the workaround.

Oregon law explicitly classifies drones as "aircraft" for pesticide purposes. That triggers the Aerial Pesticide Applicator (APA) license — and the APA has a paradox baked in.

Step 1 Pass ODA Laws & Safety + Ag category exam. Get the CPA (Commercial Pesticide Applicator) license. Easy. ~3 weeks.
→
The Wall To get the aerial endorsement (APA) you need 50 documented flight hours of pesticide application. But you can't legally apply pesticide without the APA. Chicken & egg.
→
The Workaround Log 50 hrs spraying water, fertilizer, or fire retardant on ODA's "Time in Service" form. Counts. ~3 weeks of focused flying.
⚠ This is the move

Order the drone week 1 if cash allows. Start logging hours on water sims while your federal paperwork is in the FAA queue. By the time Part 137 lands (~12 weeks), you're at 50 hrs and ready to file the APA. That's how 16 weeks happens. Skip this and you're at 30+ weeks.

▼ What nuWay's FastPass does NOT solve

FastPass ($800) lets you sub under nuWay's Part 137 while your federal app is pending. That closes the FAA gap. It does not close the Oregon APA gap — ODA explicitly says only the licensed individual can apply, even under supervision. So FastPass is useful in 30 other states, mostly useless to us here.

⚠ Insurance reality check

Standard commercial drone policies exclude chemical application. You need FAR 137 coverage with at minimum LC/RC chemical liability. Get FAC if you're doing herbicide. $4–9K/yr for a single-drone op. Carriers: BWI, SkyWatch, Falcon, Global.

§07 — The Leverage Play

The money isn't coming from our pockets.

Here's the part the turnkey sellers really don't want you to see. Once we have signed farmer LOIs in hand, we don't need $50K in cash. We walk those LOIs into a bank with my existing 1Commerce books as the guarantor, and we finance the whole damn thing.

The bank's math is stupidly simple. They look at three things: who's paying it back, what's the collateral, and is the revenue real. We hand them a win on all three.

Borrower quality: 1Commerce LLC has operating history, live Stripe revenue from the Operating Excellence Bundle tiers, existing business bank relationships, and a personal guarantor (me) with a clean track record. Your LLC is clean paper. Two-entity deal, one experienced operator. That underwrites.

Collateral: A DJI Agras T40/T50 is not a sofa. It's a titled, insurable piece of equipment with a strong resale market and a proper depreciation schedule. Banks know how to value this — AgDirect has literal loan products built for it.

Revenue certainty: This is where the LOIs flip the deal. $45–75K of pre-committed acreage for 2026 isn't a business plan — it's a receivable. When the loan officer sees three signed LOIs from named Oregon growers covering more than a full year's debt service, the deal writes itself.

Realistic ask: $50–70K total package. 10–20% down. 5–7 year amortization. Fixed or variable. Monthly payment lands somewhere around $700–1,100 — which one 400-acre hazelnut contract covers for the entire year.

▲ What a farmer LOI looks like

"I, [FARMER], operator of [FARM] in [COUNTY], OR, intend to contract with [LLC] for drone-applied [FUNGICIDE / HERBICIDE] services on approximately [N] acres during the 2026 spray season at a rate of $[XX]/acre, subject to final scheduling and regulatory licensing."

Not a contract. Not binding. Just a one-page signed statement of intent. Most farmers will sign this on a handshake at the kitchen table.
