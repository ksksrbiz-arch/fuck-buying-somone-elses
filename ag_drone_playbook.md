## 1. Executive Summary & Business Case

### The Opportunity

Oregon's Willamette Valley and surrounding ag regions (hazelnuts, vineyards, grass seed/clover, wheat) have massive unmet demand for precision drone spraying. Pratum Co-op alone managed 19,000 acres in 2023 with overflow they cannot service. Per-acre pricing ranges $14–$35 depending on crop and treatment. A single rig can realistically cover 3,000–5,000 acres in a season.

### The Problem with the Status Quo

Turnkey dealers (e.g., nuWay) sell "business-in-a-box" rigs for $50K–$60K+ with heavy margins, long lead times, and limited support for the real bottlenecks: Oregon's APA (Aerial Pesticide Applicator) license and financing. Most new operators get stuck in the licensing/financing catch-22 and never launch.

### Our Edge (The Bootstrap Model)

- Used/refurbished drone (T40 or T50) + DIY trailer = $40K–$55K total startup cost vs. $50K–$60K+ turnkey.
- Oregon APA workaround: Log 50 hours on non-pesticide aerial ops (water, fertilizer, fire retardant) — completely legal and free.
- Farmer LOIs as collateral → bank financing with minimal personal cash outlay.
- Direct channels (ag retailers, co-op overflow, trade shows, USDA grants) bypass dealer markups.

### Year 1 Targets

- 3,000+ acres sprayed
- $45K–$75K+ gross revenue (conservative)
- Profitable by month 6–8
- Clear path to 2–3 rigs and $150K–$300K+ by Year 2 (add seeding, scouting, multispectral mapping)

### Core Principle

We do not save up. We turn signed farmer commitments into capital. The gating constraint is not money or hardware — it is disciplined execution of the 50-hour log + Part 137 paperwork + 3 LOIs. Both licensing steps are free. Start them this week.

## 3. Regulatory & Licensing Roadmap (Oregon-Specific)

### The Catch-22 (And How We Break It)

Oregon requires an Aerial Pesticide Applicator (APA) license for commercial spraying. To get it you need 50 hours of logged aerial pesticide application — but you can't legally spray pesticides until you have the license. Classic chicken-and-egg.

### The Legal Workaround (50-Hour Non-Pesticide Log)

You may log 50 hours of aerial application of water, fertilizer, or fire retardant under FAA Part 107 / 137 exemptions. This counts toward the Oregon APA requirement. Many operators have successfully used this path.

### Step-by-Step Licensing Sequence

**Phase 1 – Federal Foundation (Weeks 1–4)**

1. Obtain FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate (study + exam, ~$175, 2–3 weeks).
2. Form or designate the LLC that will hold the Part 137 Certificate of Waiver or Authorization (CWA) — usually a separate entity from your main 1Commerce LLC for liability isolation.
3. Apply for FAA Part 137 (Agricultural Aircraft Operation) — requires proof of insurance, aircraft registration, and pilot qualifications. Use the standard template.

**Phase 2 – Oregon APA (Weeks 4–10)**

1. Order ODA (Oregon Department of Agriculture) study materials and schedule exams (Core + Aerial categories, ~$58 each).
2. Begin 50-hour logging immediately on non-pesticide ops (water on test plots, fertilizer on cover crops, fire retardant training with local fire districts).
3. Log every flight with date, location, purpose, hours, aircraft N-number, and pilot. Use the official ODA log format.
4. Once 50 hours are logged and exams passed, submit APA application with logbook + proof of insurance.

**Phase 3 – Insurance & Bonding (Parallel, Weeks 6–12)**

- Minimum: $1M–$2M general liability + chemical drift coverage.
- Expect $4,000–$9,000/year for a single-rig operation.
- Shop via: State Farm Ag, Nationwide Ag, or specialty brokers familiar with drones (many now understand Part 107/137 ops).

### Timeline Reality Check

Aggressive but achievable: First legal spray in 16–20 weeks if you start logging hours and paperwork this week. Many people drag this out 6–12 months because they treat it as a side project.

## 4. Equipment & Rig Build (DIY vs Turnkey)

### Recommended Platforms (2026 Pricing)

| Platform | Payload | Acres/Hour (Typical) | Best For | Price (Used/New) | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DJI T40 (used) | 40L | 35–50 | High-volume spraying | $18K–$26K | Best value entry |
| DJI T50 (new) | 50L | 45–65 | Larger fields, faster | $32K–$38K | Future-proof |
| DJI Agras T30 | 30L | 25–40 | Smaller/vineyard work | $12K–$18K (used) | Backup / niche |

### Rig Build – DIY Trailer ($3,000–$5,000 vs $18K–$25K turnkey)

**Essential Components**

- 7x14 or 7x16 enclosed trailer (used, $2,500–$4,000)
- 5,000–7,000W generator (Honda or Generac, $1,200–$2,000)
- Battery charging station (4–6 batteries simultaneous, $800–$1,500)
- Water tank + mixing system (300–500 gal, $600–$1,200)
- Tool storage, spare parts, PPE cabinet
- LED work lights + backup camera

### Build Philosophy

Keep it simple, mobile, and over-built on the electrical side. The biggest downtime killer is charging speed and generator reliability. Spend money on good batteries and a quiet, reliable generator.

### Insurance Requirements

- Aircraft hull + liability (required for Part 137)
- General liability + chemical drift
- Commercial auto for the trailer/truck
- Workers' comp once you have employees/contractors

### Startup Cost Breakdown (Target: $40K–$55K Total)

| Category | Low | High | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drone (used T40 or new T50) | $18,000 | $32,000 | Refurbished T40 is sweet spot |
| Batteries + chargers (4–6) | $4,000 | $8,000 | Buy extras — downtime is expensive |
| Generator (5–7kW) | $1,200 | $2,200 | Quiet inverter model |
| DIY Trailer + build | $3,000 | $5,500 | Enclosed + electrical + plumbing |
| FAA Part 107 + ODA exams | $300 | $500 | Study materials + fees |
| Insurance (Year 1) | $4,000 | $9,000 | Shop aggressively |
| Tools, PPE, misc | $2,000 | $4,000 | Calibration kit, mixing station |
| **Total** | **$40K** | **$55K** | Target: $47K median |

### Financing Strategy (The Leverage Play)

**Step 1 – Get 3 Signed LOIs (Letters of Intent)**

Farmers commit in writing to X acres at $Y/acre for the upcoming season. This is not a contract, but it is bankable collateral.

**Step 2 – Use LOIs for Bank Financing**

- AgDirect (John Deere Credit) — very drone-friendly, 2–7 year terms, 20% down typical.
- AgWest / Farm Credit — relationship-based, flexible.
- SBA 7(a) via local credit union — good for first-time operators.
- Example: $50K rig with $10K down + 3 LOIs covering 1,500+ acres → ~$400–$550/month payment.

**Step 3 – Bootstrap Cash Flow**

First 60–90 days of spraying revenue covers operating costs and begins paying down the note. Many operators are cash-flow positive by month 4–5.

> **Rule:** Never put more than $15–20K of personal capital at risk. The rest is financed against committed revenue.

## 6. Farmer Acquisition System (The Real Moat)

### Primary Channels (Ranked by Effectiveness)

**Ag Retailer Partnerships (Highest ROI)**  
Wilbur-Ellis, Nutrien, Simplot, local co-ops  
Approach as "overflow partner" — they keep the relationship, you do the work, they take a small finder's fee or none.  
Goal: Become the preferred drone contractor for 3–5 retailers in Year 1.

**Pratum Co-op Overflow**  
They publicly cannot service all demand. Walk in with proof of insurance + license and ask for referrals. Many will send work your way.

**Trade Shows & Events**  
Oregon Wine Symposium (February) — vineyard owners  
PNW Ag Expo, Oregon Farm Bureau events  
Booth or just walk the floor with business cards and LOI template.

**Direct Outreach (LOI Blitz)**  
Target 50–100 farmers in your county via county assessor lists or farm bureau directories.  
Script: *"I'm launching a precision drone spraying service focused on hazelnuts/vineyards. I'm offering early-season pricing and guaranteed slot if you sign an LOI this week. No obligation beyond the letter."*

### LOI Template (One-Page, Simple)

- Farmer name, farm name, contact
- Crop(s) and approximate acres
- Desired treatment window
- Price per acre (or "market rate at time of service")
- Signature + date
- *"This letter of intent is non-binding and for planning purposes only."*

> **Target: 3 signed LOIs in the first 30 days.** This is the single most important leading indicator of launch success.

## 7. 16-Week Launch Timeline (Week-by-Week)

**Weeks 1–2: Validate & Commit**
- Get 3 signed LOIs from target farmers (hazelnuts or vineyards preferred).
- Order Part 107 study guide + block study time.
- Decide which LLC will hold the Part 137 CWA.
- Pull 12 months of personal/business bank statements for lenders.
- Choose primary lender (AgDirect vs local credit union).

**Weeks 3–5: License Stack Begins**
- Take and pass Part 107 exam.
- Submit Part 137 application (aircraft registration + insurance proof).
- Begin 50-hour non-pesticide logging (water/fertilizer on test plots or cover crops).
- Order drone + batteries (used T40 is recommended starting point).
- Shop insurance quotes (aim for binder by Week 8).

**Weeks 6–9: Hardware + Logging**
- Take delivery of drone + build DIY trailer.
- Continue logging hours aggressively (target 8–12 hours/week).
- Finalize ODA exam prep and schedule Core + Aerial exams.
- Begin soft outreach to 2–3 ag retailers for overflow conversations.

**Weeks 10–13: Federal + Oregon APA**
- Complete 50-hour log + submit APA application to ODA.
- Receive Part 137 CWA from FAA.
- Finalize insurance policy.
- Conduct first test flights with water on non-crop areas (calibration, safety).

**Weeks 14–16: First Spray Ready**
- Receive Oregon APA license (or temporary authorization).
- Confirm 3–5 farmer contracts from LOIs.
- Load first chemical loads and conduct supervised test sprays.
- Execute first paid commercial spray (target: Week 16–18).
- Celebrate. Then immediately start booking Year 2 expansion.

### Key Milestones & Go/No-Go Gates

- **Week 4:** 3 signed LOIs + Part 107 passed → Continue.
- **Week 8:** Drone + trailer operational + insurance bound → Continue.
- **Week 12:** 50 hours logged + APA application submitted → Green light for first spray.

> If any gate is missed, the timeline slips 3–6 weeks. Ruthless weekly accountability is required.

## 8. Operations, Safety & Compliance

### Daily Operations Checklist

- **Pre-flight:** Battery health, propellers, spray system calibration, weather (wind <10 mph, no rain forecast).
- **Chemical handling:** Follow label exactly, use proper PPE, triple-rinse system between products.
- **Recordkeeping:** Every flight logged with acres, product, rate, weather, pilot, aircraft.
- **Post-flight:** Equipment inspection, battery charging, trailer secure.

### Safety Non-Negotiables

- No spraying near people, livestock, or sensitive crops without buffer.
- Drift mitigation: Use drift-reducing nozzles, fly at proper height, avoid temperature inversions.
- Emergency procedures: Spill kit, first-aid, contact list for ODA/FAA.

### Recordkeeping (Legal Requirement)

- Maintain 3 years of flight logs, chemical application records, and maintenance logs.
- Be audit-ready at all times — ODA and FAA both conduct random inspections.
